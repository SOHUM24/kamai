The problem: income that moves, credit that does not

CTDM U1

Problem statement

India's gig, platform and informal self-employed workers (7.7 million gig workers in 2020-21, projected 23.5 million by 2029-30) are refused formal credit or fail to repay it. Banks need a bureau score and payslip these workers lack, and then apply fixed monthly EMIs to income that arrives weekly and unevenly. With no savings buffer, any shock converts into default or into informal debt at rates far above bank APR. The bank loses a growing segment and the workers lose compounding income.

1 Access

No payslip, no ITR, thin bureau file. Result: rejection before assessment.

2 Structure

Fixed 30-day EMI against weekly payouts. Result: cash-flow mismatch.

3 Resilience

No buffer. Result: one fuel or repair shock becomes a missed instalment.

The three failures compound: no access pushes workers to informal lenders, informal debt drains the buffer, and no buffer breaks any fixed schedule. Existing products attack at most one.

Root cause: fishbone and 5 Whys

5 Whys on the top root cause

1 Workers miss EMIs. 2 Instalment falls due in a low-income week. 3 Schedule is fixed at 30 days. 4 Product was priced for salaried monthly income. 5 The bank treats the month, not the week, as the unit of credit. Kamai targets this.

The product: three layers, one line of credit

CTDM U2

Kamai is a regulated, bank-issued, revolving micro-credit line plus linked recurring savings account, underwritten on verified cash flows, with repayment that flexes automatically when income shocks are detected.

L1 Underwrite

Consent via Account Aggregator for 6 to 12 months of statements. Score uses average inflow, volatility (CV), payout sources, platform share, balance floor and obligation ratio. Bureau score is secondary, never a rejection trigger.

Limit = 1.2 x median monthly net inflow x (1 - CV)

Example: 1.2 x Rs 28,000 x (1 - 0.35) = Rs 21,840. First loan capped at Rs 25,000 Assumption

L2 Buffer

Opt-out auto-sweep of 3 to 5% of every payout into named buckets: "Bike repair", "Rain-day fund". Streak counter, loss-framed nudges. Target: 2 to 4 weeks of inflow in 9 to 12 months.

So what: the buffer is the first repayment source, so a shock does not reach the bank first.

L3 Adapt

If weekly inflow falls 25 to 30% below the 3-month median for 2 weeks, Kamai shifts the due date, moves to interest-only, or draws the buffer, with consent given at onboarding. Portfolio engine stress-tests fuel spike, payout cut, monsoon, outage and rule change.

Journey

1 Consent (AA) → 2 Cash-flow score in minutes → 3 Line set, Key Fact Statement with APR shown → 4 Draw to own bank account → 5 Auto-sweep to buckets → 6 Shock detected, schedule adjusts, SMS in Hindi.

Governance: RBI Digital Lending directions: direct disbursal, APR disclosed, no hidden fees, cooling-off, grievance officer, data in India, consent-based use. First-loss guarantee, if a fintech partner is used, within the RBI 5% cap. Team to re-verify current text before the panel.

How we thought: SWOT, ideas, lateral move, six hats

  • T Fintech apps at 12 to 36% APR with minute-level approval

Assumption Design change: lean onboarding, vernacular UI.

Brainstorm funnel

42 ideas → 6 clusters (data, repayment, savings, trust, channel, risk) → 3 shortlisted: weekly repayment unit, buffer-first repayment, platform payout sweep.

Lateral move

Treat the weekly payout as the unit of credit and make the buffer the first repayment source. This reversed the product from "loan then savings" to "savings protects the loan".

Evidence and denominator test (Levitin)

Claim: "gig workers earn Rs 40,000 a month". Source: platform press release. Incentive: recruitment. Sample: top earners. Denominator: active drivers, not all. Rejected. Kamai sizes limits from each worker's own verified inflow instead. Correlation caution: higher platform share correlates with lower default, but tenure on platform may drive both, so the pilot tests both separately.
